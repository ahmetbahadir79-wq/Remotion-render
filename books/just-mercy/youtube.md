What do you do when you find out the guy on death row is innocent — and the state already knows?

This is the story at the heart of Just Mercy by Bryan Stevenson. Walter McMillian sat on Alabama's death row before his trial even started, convicted on testimony the prosecution knew was coerced. But this book isn't just about one wrongful conviction — it's a structural indictment of what happens when a legal system decides some people are beyond redemption.

We break down every major case — from coerced witnesses to children sentenced to die in prison to women prosecuted for poverty — and trace the argument Stevenson builds: that mercy isn't weakness, it's the only force that can interrupt a machine designed never to look back at its own mistakes.
